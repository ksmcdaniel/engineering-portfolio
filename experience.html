Project #1: Micro-fluidic Device Fabrication

Overview: Established proof of concept for a novel optoporation-based drug delivery method for suspension cells. My role was designing and fabricating four different microfluidic device geometries to optimize cell residence time and promote mixing between the solvent and the cells. Lymphoblast cells were irradiated with a femtosecond laser, opening temporary membrane pores and enabling delivery of 40 kDa FITC-Dextran.

Skills Learned: I developed photomasks in AutoCAD and learned how to fabricate microfluidic devices in the cleanroom using photolithography. I characterized device properties using optical profilometry. I also learned how to align and collimate an infrared laser, gaining familarity with optical tools like dichroic mirrors and beam profilers. Finally, I learned how to passage cells and analyze cell transfection using flow cytometry.

Project #2: Liquid Crystal Elastomers for Biosensing

Overview: Synthesized liquid crystal elastomers that exhibited color changes in response to mechanical deformation. Optimized the color response by changing the ratio of mesogen to cross-linker and altering UV photo-polymerization conditions. Presented work at the Harvard Chemistry and Chemical Biology Research Symposium in 2025 and won best poster.

Skills Learned: I learned how to use levers such as dopants, mesogen:crosslinker ratio, and UV intensity to optimize the outcome of a two-step photopolymerization reaction. I explored the thickness dependence of liquid crystal alignment and experimented with different surface treatments to increase alignment uniformity and therefore color sensivity. I also learned how to take SEM images to analyze molecular structures within the elastomers.

Electrical Engineering Lead

Harvard Humanitarian Initative · September 2025 - December 2025

Overview: Served as electrical team lead on a project developing a digital tally counter for the Harvard Humanitarian Initiative. Met with the client to determine electrical specifications and led a team of five to design circuitry.

Design Specifications

160 mm x 80 mm Total footprint
8+ hours Battery life
10 MB Flash memory
2 - 3 m BLE connection range
60 mWh Energy supply
8 Data buttons

The primary electrical challenge was meeting size and power constraints while supporting multiple user inputs and peripherals. For the device to be applicable in the field, it had to have 8+ hours of battery life and 8 buttons in a handheld formfactor. However, to improve the usability of the device, the clients also wanted haptics such as an LED array and a resonant motor, as well as a microSD port for data storage. Even after using I2C buses to wire our peripherals, we still only had two pins left over for the 5 data collection buttons as well as an ON/OFF, save, and reset button.

The solution was to implement analog resistor ladders, which allowed us to map each button press to a distinct analog voltage (or range) on our MCU’s input pins. We were able to wire all the buttons using only 2 GPIO pins. We used decoupling capacitors and fixed external pull-up resistors to improve the robustness of the circuit and wrote calibration code to assign each button to an analog voltage. The final prototype circuit was made on a solderable breadboard with schematics for a custom PCB.

My other main contribution was designing the device’s mechanical shell in Fusion 360, based on measurements I took of the electrical circuit. I made a flexible snap fit design and integrated space for O-rings to waterproof the shell. Ultimately, the device met all functional requirements and was delivered on schedule. This experience taught me how to work with others in a technical setting and how to design under real-world constraints.
