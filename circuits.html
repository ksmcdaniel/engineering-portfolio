DAC out of Discrete Transistors

Overview: Using FETs as voltage-controlled switches and programming an Arduino to create different square wave patterns. The first op amp is an inverting summer, which adds currents coming from the four inputs and converts them to a voltage. The second op amp is simply an inverter and can be tuned to provide some gain.

LEFT: Close-up of DAC input circuitry with FETs as voltage-controlled switches. CENTER: 4 DAC inputs creating 16 possible output combinations. RIGHT: Voltage ladder showing 16 DAC outputs.

ADC out of Discrete Transistors

Overview: I took my DAC and added a comparator op amp with the inverting input connected to a fixed voltage source (Vin) and the non-inverting input connected to the DAC output. hen the DAC output was greater than Vin, the ADC output would hit VDD, since the circuit has such high gain. When the DAC output was less than Vin, the ADC output would hit ground.

The point on the DAC input ladder when the ADC transitions from ground to VDD depends on the value of Vin.

AM Radio Simulation

Overview: I used LTspice to simulate an amplitude modulating circuit in a radio, using simple 20kHz and 10kHz sine waves as my signal and 500 kHz and 1500 kHz carrier frequencies.

The first stage of the circuit applied a DC offset of 0.75V to both the signal and the carrier sine waves to keep the amplitudes bounded. The op amp series that follows essentially multiplies the two inputs together (Vout = V1 * V2), which creates an AM signal with a high carrier frequency and a low envelop frequency as shown above on the right. The signals have a low frequency component that distorts them so we add a high pass filter at the end to achieve a clean signal.

The next stage of the circuits adds the AM signals from the two channels together. There is a second op amp after the summer to provide some gain. The final, dual-channel AM signal is shown above on the right.

Finally, we have second order filters to separate the high and low carrier frequencies from each other (left). The AM signals then run through an envelope tracking circuit to kill the high frequency carrier and recover the original signals. We run the output through a low pass filter to smooth the signal (right) and then can put it through a gain stage to amplify it before sending it to a speaker. This is how an AM radio with multiple channels works!

Audio Amplifier

Overview: Non-inverting input amplifier with tunable gain using a potentiometer and built-in frequency filtering.

The first stage is a non-inverting input amplifier with a fixed and a variable feedback resistor for tunable gain. The second stage is a second-order high pass filter to isolate the higher frequencies of our song. The third stage provides some gain before the signal is sent to the speaker.
